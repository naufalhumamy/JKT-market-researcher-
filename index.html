Jakarta Export Market Update — Week 42, 13 October 2026

Coverage: news published 3–13 October 2026. Items dated 1–2 October are marked "context". Numbers in [brackets] refer to the Sources list. Text marked "Analysis:" is our interpretation, not a sourced fact.

Summary

  • Palm oil: Indonesian exports rose while Malaysian stocks hit a record. GAPKI reports Indonesian palm product exports of 3.59Mt in August (+12.56% MoM), with refined products up 15.12% [1][2]. Malaysia's September stocks reached a record 3.51Mt (+24.54% MoM) [3]. Even so, Malaysian futures recovered to about MYR 4,650 on 12 Oct [4].
  • Rubber is at a 13-year high. Futures rose above 260 US cents/kg (9 Oct) and were at 264.80 on 12 Oct (+12.2% MoM, +53.6% YoY), driven by Thai flooding, rain disruption and El Niño risk. Chinese tyre demand remains soft [5].
  • Cocoa is rising again on West African risk. The October cocoa bean reference price is US$6,057.87/MT (+7.49%), with a 7.5% export duty and 7.5% levy [6][7]. ICE futures were back above US$5,600/t on 12 Oct [8].
  • Tanjung Priok is running normally. We found no in-window reports of congestion, strikes or system outages. The rupiah weakened slightly to Rp17,913/USD on 12 Oct [9]. Trade Expo Indonesia (TEI) runs 14–18 Oct at ICE BSD in Tangerang, just inside the Priok hinterland [10][11].

Commodity Reports

1. Palm Oil & Shortening (CPO, refined olein, oleochemicals)

Market Overview - August exports (GAPKI, reported 9 Oct) - Total palm product exports were 3.59Mt (+12.56% MoM). Refined products were 2.64Mt (+15.12%), CPO 329kt (+31.83%) and oleochemicals 501kt (–6.74%) [1][2]. - Export value was US$4.29bn in August and US$27.55bn in Jan–Aug (+11.14% YoY). Jan–Aug volume was 23.37Mt (+2.97% YoY) [1][2]. - Indonesian stocks fell to 2.21Mt at end-August [1]. - Malaysia (MPOB, 12 Oct): September stocks were a record 3.51Mt (+24.54% MoM), production was 2.12Mt (+16.48%) and exports were 1.12Mt (–12.13%) [3][4]. - Prices - Malaysian futures rose about 1.3% to around MYR 4,650 on 12 Oct, supported by Dalian prices and a 33.5% rise in Malaysian shipments in 1–10 Oct versus the same period of September (Intertek) [4][12]. - Futures gained almost 3% in the week to 9 Oct [12]. - Context (3 Oct): futures fell in the week to 2 Oct, with the October contract down RM121 to RM4,354 [13].

Direction: Shanghai and Shekou (China). Shortening and refined olein are among our steadiest Jakarta–Shanghai cargoes. - GAPKI's August data shows the largest MoM increases going to Pakistan, India, Russia, Bangladesh and Malaysia [1]. China was not named among the gainers. - Analysis: China is not a growth driver this month. The China lanes depend on existing contract flows and Q4 bakery and food restocking ahead of Chinese New Year (6 Feb 2027).

Key Market Drivers - Strong refined-product exports. Refined products are the category shortening falls under, and they rose 15.12% MoM in August [2]. - Firm Asian demand in early October. Indian refiners reportedly booked about 150kt in three days, and Malaysian shipments recovered strongly in 1–10 Oct [12]. - Lower Indonesian stocks. Stocks fell to 2.21Mt, leaving less surplus to push out at a discount [1].

Market Challenges - Higher export cost. For October, the CPO reference price is US$1,042.15/MT (+3.44%), the export duty US$178/MT and the levy 12.5% (US$130.27/MT) [7]. - Cheaper Malaysian supply. Record Malaysian stocks (3.51Mt) mean Malaysia has every reason to discount, which could pull Chinese buyers away from Indonesian product [3][4]. - More domestic biodiesel use. Domestic biodiesel use was 1.35Mt in August (+5.03%). GAPKI expects full-year export volume to fall about 3.8% as domestic absorption grows [2].

Government Policy - October duty and levy: CPO duty US$178/MT and levy 12.5%, both based on the reference price. Branded packaged RBD olein (25kg or less) carries a US$48/MT duty under Kepmendag 1922/2026 [7]. - B50: Fortune IDN (9 Oct) says the B50 programme "takes effect in October" [2]. Other reports give a different start date (see Unverified). - November reference price: due around the end of October. It is the next cost signal for Q4 export pricing.

Market Trend & Outlook (Q4 2026) — Analysis: - Expect steady volumes with margin pressure. Malaysia's record stocks should cap regional prices, while Indonesia's duty and levy follow its own (higher) reference price. That squeezes Indonesian refiners' margins on China-bound shortening. - Volumes into Shanghai/Shekou should hold through November–December on seasonal restocking. The downside risk is Chinese buyers splitting orders toward Malaysian product. - Swing factors: the November reference price, and China's September trade data due this week [12].

2. Natural Rubber (SIR / TSR20, block rubber)

Market Overview - On 9 Oct, rubber futures rose above 260 US cents/kg, the highest since early 2013 [5]. - On 12 Oct the price was 264.80 US cents/kg (+2.24% on the day, +12.2% MoM, +53.6% YoY) [5]. - Context (1 Oct): prices had already returned to about 260 cents on rain disruption, low all-steel tyre inventories and higher oil prices raising synthetic rubber costs [5].

Direction: Japan main ports and Busan/Incheon (tyre makers), plus Shanghai/Shekou (China). - Analysis: Japan is traditionally a top buyer of Indonesian block rubber. Most Indonesian rubber, however, loads from Sumatra (Belawan, Palembang, Panjang) rather than Priok. Jakarta-origin volume is mainly West Java and Banten production and transhipment.

Key Market Drivers - Thai supply disruption. Flooding in Thailand is disrupting output [5]. - In Chonburi, 34 plants at Amata City stopped production after a flood embankment collapsed, more than 10 of them auto-parts suppliers. - The Federation of Thai Industries' preliminary loss estimate rose to almost 6bn baht. Suppliers expected to restart from 12 Oct [14]. - Weather. Continued rain and El Niño risk are limiting tapping in major producing countries [5]. - Higher synthetic rubber costs from higher oil prices make natural rubber relatively more attractive (context, 1 Oct) [5].

Market Challenges - Soft Chinese demand. Chinese tyre demand is "subdued", and Chinese tyre makers were on holiday in early October [5]. - Buyer resistance at 13-year highs. Analysis: buyers may delay purchases at these prices. - Rupiah. The rupiah closed at Rp17,913/USD on 12 Oct, a 14-point drop [9]. Analysis: a weak rupiah supports Indonesian exporters' USD price competitiveness.

Government Policy - We found no new Indonesian rubber export rule in the window. - Permendag 1/2026 on natural rubber exports remains in force (not in window, background only).

Market Trend & Outlook (Q4 2026) — Analysis: - Prices should stay firm. Q4 is the seasonal low-output ("wintering") period in Thailand and Vietnam. Combined with flood losses, prices are likely to stay historically high through Q4. - Value up, volume uncertain. For Sinokor, high prices raise cargo value but not necessarily volume. Indonesian smallholders tap more at high prices, but rain limits output. - China is the swing factor. If Chinese restocking returns after the holidays, Japan- and Korea-bound volumes from Jakarta should stay steady. Otherwise, expect range-bound trading.

3. Cocoa (beans and processed cocoa: butter, powder, liquor)

Market Overview - On 12 Oct, ICE cocoa was at US$5,665.89/t, still down 5.93% MoM but nearly double the 27 Feb three-year low of US$2,888 [8]. - Price path in the window - 5 Oct: futures rose 3.47% to about US$5,867, a two-week high [15]. - 7 Oct: eased to about US$5,640 [8]. - 12 Oct: rose again on West African crop risk [8]. - Indonesian reference prices (October): the cocoa bean reference price is US$6,057.87/MT (+7.49%) and the export check price (HPE) is US$5,686/MT (+7.88%) [6][7].

Direction: Shanghai and Shekou (China), with some processed cocoa to Japan and Korea. - Analysis: Indonesian cocoa exports are mostly processed products from grinders in Java, Sulawesi and Batam. The Jakarta-relevant share is cocoa butter, powder and liquor from West Java and Banten grinders.

Key Market Drivers - West African supply risk. StoneX expects Ivory Coast 2026/27 output to fall 11% to 1.8Mt and Ghana's to fall about 13% [8]. - Logistics problems in Ivory Coast. Heavy rain has damaged roads and bridges and slowed deliveries to port [15]. - Ghana farmgate price. Ghana raised its farmgate price about 2.4% to roughly US$3,647/t, which increases the risk of cross-border smuggling [15].

Market Challenges - High ICE stocks. ICE stocks are at a 2.25-year high (3.53m bags), and Ivory Coast port arrivals are +19.8% YoY this season [15]. - Export cost. Indonesia's 7.5% export duty plus 7.5% levy on beans raises the cost of exporting beans [6][7]. Analysis: this favours exporting processed products over beans.

Government Policy - October 2026: cocoa bean export duty 7.5% (PMK 38/2024 as amended by PMK 68/2025) and export levy 7.5% (PMK 69/2025 as amended by PMK 9/2026) [6]. - TEI 2026 (14–18 Oct) has a dedicated coffee, tea and cocoa hall [11].

Market Trend & Outlook (Q4 2026) — Analysis: - Prices should stay volatile but firm while El Niño risk persists. - The Q3 grinding data due 15 Oct will show whether demand is recovering [8]. Weak grindings would cap prices. - Watch the November cocoa reference price. It will likely rise again if ICE stays above about US$5,600, adding to bean export costs. Expect stable processed-cocoa container volumes from Jakarta.

Port & Competitor Brief

Tanjung Priok - We found no in-window reports of congestion, strikes, closures or INSW/CEISA outages at JICT, KOJA, NPCT1 or MAL. - Weather: BMKG warned of waves up to 4m in some Indonesian waters for 10–13 Oct. The Java Sea was named for high wind speeds but given no wave-height warning [16]. - ODOL (overloaded trucks): Kemenhub plans enforcement at ports as part of "Zero ODOL 2027". The first trial is at Tanjung Perak (Surabaya), not Priok, and no dates were given [17].

Intra-Asia market (Linerlytica) - 5 Oct: congestion rose over the previous three weeks at Singapore, Port Klang and Ho Chi Minh/Cai Mep. Feeder capacity is tight in the Bangkok-max segment [18]. - 13 Oct: backlogs at Chinese ports eased during National Day, but delays continue in Southeast and South Asia [19].

Thailand (Laem Chabang) - Laem Chabang is operating. The disruption is landside: the Lat Krabang–Laem Chabang rail shuttle is suspended and road moves are slower [20]. - Navia Freight (5 Oct) cited forecasts of more rain on 9–13 Oct [20].

Competitors - We found no new in-window service, rotation, blank sailing or surcharge news touching Jakarta for KMTC, HMM, CK Line, Namsung, SITC, COSCO, Jin Jiang, ONE, RCL, Interasia or Gold Star Line. - Coming up: the HMM/PIL/RCL North China–Indonesia loop (Qingdao–Shanghai–Nansha–Jakarta) starts 8 Nov (reported in Week 41). - Not touching Jakarta: - COSCO's new Shekou–Nansha–Port Klang "Easy Buy Service" (7 Oct) [21]. - The COSCO/OOCL/X-Press Nansha–Laem Chabang–Yangpu loop (context, 2 Oct) [22]. - Suez return: Linerlytica says ONE and HMM are the next carriers expected to return to Suez routing [19]. Analysis: this is East–West tonnage and has no direct effect on our lanes, but the cascade could add intra-Asia capacity over time.

Implications for Sinokor Jakarta (Analysis)

  • Palm and shortening to China. Indonesian exporters face rising duty and levy costs while Malaysia discounts from record stocks. Sales should keep in close contact with Shanghai/Shekou shortening and olein shippers to protect existing contracts and spot any order-splitting toward Malaysia early.
  • Rubber value at a 13-year high. Japan- and Korea-bound rubber shippers in the West Java and Banten hinterland may push shipments forward while prices are high. Check with key rubber and tyre accounts about Q4 volumes, and watch for Chinese restocking after the holidays.
  • Southeast Asian port delays. Congestion at Ho Chi Minh/Cai Mep and Thai landside flood disruption are relevant to our HCM and Laem Chabang lanes. Give shippers realistic transit expectations and watch for schedule slippage.
  • TEI 2026 (14–18 Oct, BSD). This is a good chance to meet exporters of furniture, food and agriculture, coffee, cocoa and manufactured goods in the Priok hinterland, and to identify new shippers for the China, Japan and Korea lanes.

Jakarta Export Market Update — Week 41, 5 October 2026

Coverage: news published 25 Sep – 5 Oct 2026, plus the latest BPS monthly release (August 2026 data, released 1 Oct 2026). Source numbers in [brackets] refer to the Sources list.

1. Key Takeaways

  • Indonesia's August exports rose 6.72% YoY to US$26.61bn, led by manufacturing (+12.48% YoY), nickel (+51.67%), iron & steel (+20.38%) and aluminium (+206.78%) [1][2][3]. By our calculation that is about +1.5% vs July (US$26.22bn) [4].
  • Exports from DKI Jakarta province fell 19.88% YoY to US$1.18bn in August, dragged down by jewellery/precious metals (–US$94.13m) and footwear (–US$76.31m). Jan–Aug exports to Thailand were +57.15% and to China +0.23% [5][6].
  • More competition is coming on Jakarta–China. HMM, PIL and RCL launch a new North China–Indonesia loop (Qingdao–Shanghai–Nansha–Jakarta–Surabaya–Singapore), with the first sailing from Qingdao on 8 Nov 2026. Meratus has started a Shanghai–Nansha–Ho Chi Minh–Jakarta service [15][16].
  • Tanjung Priok is running normally. We found no reports of congestion or outages this week. IPC TPK's Priok terminal volume is up 13.2% year to date [14]. Golden Week (1–7 Oct) is softening China-bound demand in the short term [17].

Strong - Nickel & products: +51.67% YoY in August [1]. - Iron & steel (HS 72): +20.38% YoY [1]. - Aluminium (HS 76): +206.78% YoY [1]. - Manufactured goods overall: +12.48% YoY [3]. - Fats & oils (HS 15): the largest non-oil surplus contributor in Jan–Aug, at US$24.91bn [7].

  • China Golden Week (1–7 Oct). Linerlytica reports cargo volumes falling after the Mid-Autumn holiday and carriers discounting October bookings [17]. Sea-Intelligence (24 Sep, context) warned that mid-to-late October schedules are "highly volatile placeholders" [25].

  • US palm oil compliance. On 29 Sep, US CBP issued Withhold Release Orders on palm oil from PT Mitra Aneka Rezeki and PT Hardaya Inti Plantation [26]. This is not a Sinokor lane, but it adds compliance pressure on Indonesian palm exporters.

10. Implications & Suggested Actions for Sinokor Jakarta (Analysis)

  • China lane defence. From late November, the HMM/PIL/RCL loop will offer a direct Jakarta–Shanghai/Nansha call alongside existing KCI-type services. Sinokor should secure Q4 commitments with key Shanghai/Shekou shippers (steel products, chemicals, CPO derivatives) before the new capacity starts.
  • Golden Week lull. Expect softer China-bound bookings in early October as carriers discount. Target shippers holding cargo for the post-holiday restart (from 8 Oct), and watch for competitor blank sailings that could release cargo.
  • Thailand opportunity. DKI-origin exports to Thailand are up 57% YTD. Identify the commodities and shippers behind the increase and check the Laem Chabang inland flood impact before quoting transit times.
  • Korea/Japan. Busan yard pressure around Chuseok and the softer Japan PMI justify close schedule communication with Korea- and Japan-bound shippers. We see no demand-side downgrade yet.
